# Grader Calibration - reference good vs slop

Codex owns this file. Crina's judge must calibrate against these examples when scoring content and visual work.

The judge should be strict, but not timid. Operator-approved investment language is allowed. The judge must punish invented numbers, generic filler, wrong brand framing, weak CTAs, and visuals that do not carry a business message.

## How To Score

- 90+ means: strong enough to show the operator with confidence. It has a specific hook, brand-specific angle, clear audience, proof/source anchor, and a CTA that moves the business.
- 80-89 means: usable direction, but one important weakness remains.
- 70-79 means: average AI marketing. It may be readable, but it is not sharp enough for this operator.
- Below 70 means: slop. Generic, wrong frame, weak hook, fake proof, wrong visual modality, or no conversion path.

## GridFactory

### Strong Content Examples - 90+

1. Hook: "Everyone is buying AI exposure. Very few can own the machines selling compute."
   Why it earns 90+: It frames GridFactory as AI infrastructure ownership, not generic AI hype. It creates investor tension and points toward the GRID Unit/CUG offer.

2. Hook: "Plug into the park, not the grid: why AI compute is becoming a power-access game."
   Why it earns 90+: It uses the ACRaaS deck language, explains the bottleneck, and makes green-energy parks central instead of treating them as ESG decoration.

3. Hook: "$100 does not build a data center. But a GRID Unit can let a small investor enter the compute layer."
   Why it earns 90+: It speaks directly to the retail/crypto audience, uses the small-ticket hook, and connects capital limits to the lease-to-own infrastructure model.

### Strong Visual Examples - 90+

1. A cinematic container data center inside a wind/solar energy park, open racks visible, premium dark finance lighting.
   Why it earns 90+: The viewer instantly sees AI compute + energy + real infrastructure.

2. Investor flow diagram: investor -> GRID units -> GPU container -> CaaS revenue -> monthly distributions.
   Why it earns 90+: It teaches the offer in one view and supports conversion.

3. Founder talking-head in front of a deck/container/GPU-rack backdrop, explaining monthly compute income and early ownership.
   Why it earns 90+: It adds trust and makes the opportunity feel founder-led and real.

### Slop Examples - Below 70

1. "The future of AI infrastructure is here. Contact us to learn more."
   Why it fails: Generic, no ownership, no monthly income, no bottleneck, weak CTA.

2. "Data centers need power, so investors should watch this space."
   Why it fails: Too abstract. It sounds like a consultant memo and hides the offer.

3. Neon AI brain floating over a server room.
   Why it fails: Decorative AI slop. No green-energy park, no container, no ownership, no money story.

4. A flat 2D cartoon server rack with crypto coins.
   Why it fails: Makes a serious industrial finance project look cheap and unserious.

## Gulf-EL / NexRide

### Strong Content Examples - 90+

1. Hook: "Imagine Uber at inception, but EV-first, tokenised, zero-commission, and built by the community before institutions own the upside."
   Why it earns 90+: It lands the exact stage: investment/community opportunity first, ride-hailing execution second.

2. Hook: "Drivers pay the old ride-hailing model forever. NexRide is building the zero-commission alternative."
   Why it earns 90+: It makes the driver-first rebellion concrete and explains why people should care.

3. Hook: "GFEL is not a meme token with cars. It is the loyalty, staking, and asset layer behind a community mobility network."
   Why it earns 90+: It attacks the main objection directly and separates tokenised mobility from cheap crypto noise.

4. Hook: "The flywheel is simple: community funds cars, cars attract drivers, drivers bring riders, riders grow the app, the app grows the valuation."
   Why it earns 90+: It explains the business model clearly and makes the MLM/community growth thesis understandable.

### Strong Visual Examples - 90+

1. Dubai/GCC street scene with premium EV/hybrid fleet, confident driver, rider entering car, subtle app overlay tied to a real booking.
   Why it earns 90+: Real mobility, Gulf context, driver/rider trust, app credibility.

2. Tokenised mobility diagram: GFEL community -> fleet assets -> zero-commission rides -> subscriptions -> profit share/ROI incentive -> more fleet growth.
   Why it earns 90+: It explains the investment/community model and avoids fake taxi-app visuals.

3. Founder talking-head beside EV fleet or app/deck screen, explaining zero commission and community ownership.
   Why it earns 90+: Founder-led, real, and able to carry a complex investment story.

### Slop Examples - Below 70

1. "Book faster rides with the future of mobility."
   Why it fails: App-store fluff. No investor/community opportunity, no GFEL, no zero commission.

2. A generic taxi app screen with a random sedan and fake map pins.
   Why it fails: Looks like a cheap Uber clone and ignores the tokenised/community model.

3. Crypto coin flying over a car.
   Why it fails: Meme-token energy, no real fleet, no driver/rider economics.

4. Empty futuristic city with autonomous cars.
   Why it fails: Wrong stage. Gulf-EL/NexRide is EV/hybrid before robotaxi, with community investment as the current hook.

## Non-Negotiable Auto-Fails

Any of these should cap the score below 70 unless the operator specifically requested that direction:

- Generic "future of" language with no specific business mechanism.
- Weak CTAs such as "learn more", "contact us", or "request a briefing" when the campaign needs investor/community action.
- Invented numbers, cities, partners, user counts, fleet size, launch status, revenue, or regulatory claims.
- Wrong project frame: GridFactory treated as an AI app or Gulf-EL/NexRide treated as a cheap taxi app.
- Visuals with no message: decorative AI, random car stock, fake dashboards, floating app screens, or meme-token graphics.
- Copy that explains a platform but does not create desire, urgency, ownership, income, or community momentum.
- Repeating a rejected pattern: robotic hook, dry platform announcement, weak CTA, or visually empty asset.

## Operator Taste Notes

- Bold is better than timid when claims are operator-approved.
- Specific beats polished.
- Ownership and income hooks matter.
- The first line must create tension.
- A visual must carry a message even before the caption is read.
- The agent should never hide behind platform labels. "LinkedIn post about X" is not a campaign idea.
